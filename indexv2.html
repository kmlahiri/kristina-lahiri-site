About

For nearly two decades, I've watched technology evolve. From disconnected systems to APIs, automations, and now AI. The tools keep changing. The core organizational failures stay remarkably consistent: unclear accountability, subjective investment decisions, and operating structures that either block progress or fail to protect it.

What I've learned is that transformation doesn't fail because of the technology. It fails because organizations can't align decision-making, funding, and execution fast enough to keep pace with what they're deploying.

A mentor once reframed this for me using a traffic analogy that has stayed with me ever since. Drive through parts of Southeast Asia and you have complete freedom. No lanes, no signals. Every driver for themselves. And you go nowhere. The roads are full but nothing moves. Drive the Autobahn and the opposite is true: clear lanes, predictable rules, infrastructure designed for speed. No speed limit needed. The structure itself is what makes going fast possible. The guardrails aren't what slow you down. The absence of guardrails is.

That's the execution discipline I've built inside enterprises for eighteen years. It's the work that matters most now, as AI scales faster than the operating structures designed to support it.

Core Expertise

Where I create leverage

i.

Enterprise Operating Model Design

Only 35% of transformations meet their goals.1 The ones that do share a common foundation: clear decision rights, defined accountability, and governance designed before the work started. I design the operating architecture — decision rights, funding alignment, accountability structures, prioritization logic — that makes transformation governable at scale.

ii.

AI Enablement & Organizational Readiness

Over 80% of organizations using AI report no meaningful enterprise-wide impact.2 The gap isn't the technology — it's the organizational systems around it. Governance frameworks, human oversight models, accountability structures, change management, and adoption planning are what separate pilots that stick from deployments that quietly fade.

iii.

Enterprise Transformation & Integration

Large-scale transformation and M&A create the conditions where execution most commonly breaks down: fragmented accountability, competing roadmaps, and cross-functional misalignment under pressure. Leading execution across $95B+ in M&A transaction value — aligning operating models, technology roadmaps, and organizational priorities where the stakes don't allow for long learning curves.

iv.

Continuous Transformation & Digital Operations

Organizations lose an average of 42% of expected transformation value in the sustainment phase3 — after go-live, when attention shifts and the operating model wasn't designed to carry the work forward. Building the post-implementation systems that prevent that: run-the-business governance, continuous innovation cadence, adoption sustainment, and platform evolution.

v.

Transformation Execution Systems

Operating model design is only as valuable as the delivery systems that execute it. Building the machinery: portfolio governance, hybrid Agile/Waterfall frameworks, investment approval processes, and organizational cadence that keeps complex programs moving without losing accountability.

vi.

Investment Prioritization & Value Governance

Most transformation investments are prioritized by whoever lobbied hardest. Building the frameworks that replace that: structured business case development, scoring models, investment criteria, and portfolio rationalization that make funding decisions defensible and traceable to outcomes.
